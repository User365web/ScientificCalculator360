How to Use a Scientific Calculator

Understand common functions, parentheses, and order of operations.

A practical learning guide

What makes a calculator scientific?

A scientific calculator extends basic arithmetic with functions such as powers, roots, trigonometry, and logarithms. These functions are useful in algebra, physics, engineering, and many other subjects.

Use parentheses to control order

Calculations follow an order of operations: parentheses first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. For example, (2 + 3) × 4 gives 20, while 2 + 3 × 4 gives 14.

Common scientific functions

  • sin, cos, tan: trigonometric functions of an angle.
  • √x: square root of a non-negative number.
  • log(x): base-10 logarithm for positive values.
  • ln(x): natural logarithm for positive values.
  • xʸ: raise a number to a power.
  • n!: factorial of a non-negative integer.

Degrees versus radians

Trigonometric functions interpret angles differently depending on the selected mode. For example, sin(90) equals 1 in degree mode, while sin(90) in radian mode is a different value. Always check the angle mode before calculating.

Check your result

Use parentheses to make your intent clear and verify important calculations independently, especially for engineering or safety-critical work.

Trigonometry Basics: Sine, Cosine and Tangent

A starting point for working with angles and right triangles.

What is trigonometry?

Trigonometry studies relationships between angles and side lengths. In a right triangle, the sine, cosine, and tangent of an acute angle are ratios of two sides.

  • Sine: opposite side ÷ hypotenuse.
  • Cosine: adjacent side ÷ hypotenuse.
  • Tangent: opposite side ÷ adjacent side.

Degrees and radians

Angles may be measured in degrees or radians. A full turn is 360 degrees or 2π radians. The calculator's angle mode must match the unit of the angle you enter.

Example

In degree mode, sin(30) is 0.5. This is a common value worth remembering as a quick check. In radian mode, enter the angle in radians instead.

Common mistakes

Check the angle unit, use parentheses where needed, and remember that tangent is undefined at angles where cosine is zero.

Percentages and Ratios: A Practical Guide

Use simple formulas to compare quantities and calculate changes.

What is a percentage?

A percentage is a value expressed per hundred. To find p percent of a number n, calculate (p ÷ 100) × n.

Example: find 15% of 80

Calculate 0.15 × 80 = 12. So 15% of 80 is 12.

Percentage change

Percentage change is ((new value − old value) ÷ old value) × 100%. A positive result means an increase and a negative result means a decrease, provided the old value is not zero.

Ratios

A ratio compares quantities. The ratio 6:9 can be simplified by dividing both sides by 3, giving 2:3. Ratios are useful for recipes, scale drawings, mixtures, and comparing measurements.

Check units and context

Keep units consistent when comparing measurements. For percentage change, confirm the original value is the correct baseline.
